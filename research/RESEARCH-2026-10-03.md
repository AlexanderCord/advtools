The split is the usual one: **broad head terms cost $6–8.56 and carry mixed intent;
service terms cost $25–43 to enter and carry buying intent.** At a $25–43 entry bid,
$500 buys 12–20 clicks. This market is not cheap to test.

## ⚠️ Licensing and capability gates — read before acting

1. **SOC 2 attestation legally requires a licensed CPA firm.** The $766 bid on
   `soc 2 audit firms` is CPA firms bidding. A consultancy sells **readiness** —
   gap assessment, control implementation, evidence preparation — and partners with
   the attesting firm. Note that `soc 2 compliance consultant` (the readiness slice)
   is the **+829%** term, so the sellable half is also the fast-growing half.
2. **ISO 27001 certification requires an accredited certification body.** Same
   split: consultants sell implementation and readiness.
3. **CMMC** has a registered-practitioner regime (RPO / C3PAO) for parts of the
   work. **+806%** growth, 880/mo, $228 bid — compelled by US DoD contracts with a
   deadline, which is the strongest demand pattern there is. Verify what can be
   delivered without accreditation.
4. **Penetration testing is an offensive-security discipline.** Adjacent to, but
   not the same as, the SSDLC and cybersecurity audit work already evidenced. Some
   buyers expect specific certifications from the testers.

## Method notes

- **`keyword_ideas` was the wrong tool and its output was discarded.** It expands
  by Google product *category*, not by intent — security seeds returned ADT, Vivint,
  Ring and Bitdefender; cloud seeds returned "software engineer", "wake tech" and
  "surrogate agency". 420 rows, almost all noise.
- **`keyword_suggestions` is the right tool**: it returns long-tail queries that
  contain the seed phrase, which is how service-intent variants surface.
- Responses ran 60k–195k characters each, well past what fits in a working context.
  All pulls were written to disk and parsed to TSV, which is why twelve of them were
  affordable instead of two.
- `high_top_of_page_bid` is used as the budget proxy throughout; DFS's `cpc` field
  returned values above the high bid on several rows and is not trustworthy.
- Synonym collapse applied via `core_keyword` — `soc 2` / `soc-2` / `soc 2 compliant`
  are one keyword, not three. Counting seeds instead of cores would have inflated
  the SOC 2 cluster roughly threefold.
