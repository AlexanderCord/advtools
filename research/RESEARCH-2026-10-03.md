## ⚠️ Licensing and capability gates — read before acting

1. **SOC 2 attestation legally requires a licensed CPA firm.** The $766 bid on
   `soc 2 audit firms` is CPA firms bidding. A consultancy sells **readiness** —
   gap assessment, control implementation, evidence preparation — and partners with
   the attesting firm. Note that `soc 2 compliance consultant` (the readiness slice)
   is the **+829%** term, so the sellable half is also the fast-growing half.
2. **ISO 27001 certification requires an accredited certification body.** Same
   split: consultants sell implementation and readiness.
3. **CMMC** has a registered-practitioner regime (RPO / C3PAO) for parts of the
   work. **+806%** growth, 880/mo, $228 bid — compelled by US DoD contracts with a
   deadline, which is the strongest demand pattern there is. Verify what can be
   delivered without accreditation.
4. **Penetration testing is an offensive-security discipline.** Adjacent to, but
   not the same as, the SSDLC and cybersecurity audit work already evidenced. Some
   buyers expect specific certifications from the testers.

## Method notes

- **`keyword_ideas` was the wrong tool and its output was discarded.** It expands
  by Google product *category*, not by intent — security seeds returned ADT, Vivint,
  Ring and Bitdefender; cloud seeds returned "software engineer", "wake tech" and
  "surrogate agency". 420 rows, almost all noise.
- **`keyword_suggestions` is the right tool**: it returns long-tail queries that
  contain the seed phrase, which is how service-intent variants surface.
- Responses ran 60k–195k characters each, well past what fits in a working context.
  All pulls were written to disk and parsed to TSV, which is why twelve of them were
  affordable instead of two.
- `high_top_of_page_bid` is used as the budget proxy throughout; DFS's `cpc` field
  returned values above the high bid on several rows and is not trustworthy.
- Synonym collapse applied via `core_keyword` — `soc 2` / `soc-2` / `soc 2 compliant`
  are one keyword, not three. Counting seeds instead of cores would have inflated
  the SOC 2 cluster roughly threefold.

## Why each fails

**AI security and governance** — 35,800 + 32,910/mo looks large until it is split by
intent: **product 18,410/mo against service 1,330/mo.** The $55–164 bids belong to
Palo Alto, CrowdStrike, Zscaler, IBM and Microsoft. Bidding there means competing
with platform budgets for people who want to buy a platform. Best service terms:
`ai governance consultant` 140/mo, `ai governance services` 50/mo.

**Software architecture** — the apparent 263,400/mo is **one keyword counted nine
ways**: `software architecture` at 110,000/mo returned as "software of architecture",
"architecture in software", "architecture for software" and six more. The remainder
is AutoCAD, Revit and UML — *building* architecture software. Real consulting demand:
`architecture review` 260/mo at a **$6.04** bid with competition **0.01**, and
`software architecture review` at **20/mo**. Nobody is paying for this.

**Agile / scrum** — `agile transformation` returns **6 total variants**, peak 210/mo,
−66% YoY, competition 0.01. `agile transformation consultant` 90/mo, no bid recorded.

**Process automation** — 60,840/mo total, but product/tool intent takes 10,630 and
service intent only 1,250. Best: `business process automation company` 260/mo,
$22 bid, +319%.

**Data engineering** — 148,320/mo total, service intent **1,240/mo**. The bids are
genuinely strong — `data engineering consulting` **$148.85**, `data engineering
service` **$94.04 and +400%** — so there is money here. There is just almost nobody
searching. A single ad group, not a campaign.

## The rule this establishes

**Discipline-named services have almost no search demand. Compliance-named ones have
a great deal.**

The reason is the one the September research already found: compliance demand is
*compelled*. A customer's procurement, an auditor or a regulator forces someone to
go searching, with a deadline. "Process improvement", "architecture review" and
"agile transformation" are discretionary — and when a thing is discretionary and
hard to justify, people simply do not search for it.

None of these are bad capabilities. Several are core. They are just **referral and
outbound services, not paid-search offers** — which is a statement about the channel,
not about the work.

# Tooling and platform options in the security/compliance space

## 1. Do not build a compliance platform

Vanta, Drata, Secureframe and Sprinto are funded, mature and own the category. The
September rule applies: a product gap is not an opportunity unless distribution
comes with it, and here it does not.

## 2. The structural gap — platforms monitor, they do not remediate

This is the finding that matters, and it protects the consulting offer rather than
threatening it:

> Compliance platforms are **evidence collection and monitoring** tools. They connect
> to your infrastructure and track status. **They do not implement the controls.**
> Drata monitors; it does not fix your cloud misconfigurations, define your access
> review process, or advise your board.

And the sharper half:

> The platforms were designed to prove a control **exists**. They were not designed to
> verify the control **stops anything**. The 2017 SOC 2 revision and the 2022 Trust
> Services Criteria update made this explicit — auditors are now expected to test the
> **operating effectiveness** of controls, not merely their existence.

So the work that remains for a human is: design the controls, implement them, remediate
what fails, and demonstrate they actually operate. That is the readiness offer, and it
is structurally protected by the standard itself rather than by a feature gap someone
can close in a release.

## 3. Implementation partner — the distribution channel September could not find

All three majors run partner programmes aimed explicitly at compliance consultancies,
vCISOs and auditors:

- **Vanta** — Service Partner Program, partner console for managing client accounts
- **Drata** — Alliance Program, **1,300+ partners**
- **Secureframe** — referral commission or resale margin, multi-tenant portal, with
  referral and service partner tracks

This is a vendor routing customers to a consultancy. September's research concluded
distribution was the binding constraint and that marketplaces paid badly — a partner
channel where the vendor has a commercial reason to send you deals is a different
shape, and one of the few that was not screened then.

**Caveat: 1,300+ partners in Drata alone.** Being listed is not distribution. The
partners who get routed deals are the ones with a named specialism, which argues for
entering as the travel/hospitality/EdTech compliance specialist rather than as
generalist number 1,301.

Commercial terms were not published — revenue share, tiers and requirements need a
direct conversation with each vendor. **That is a £0 call and should happen before
any platform build is considered.**

## 4. Where internal tooling earns its place

Not as a product — as margin. The delivery bottleneck in readiness is evidence
collection, control mapping and remediating what the scan finds. Tooling that takes a
12-week readiness to 6 doubles throughput on a $35,000 engagement without selling a
single licence. That is the same pattern as the existing internal tooling: built for
ourselves first.

**The one genuinely open product gap is operating-effectiveness testing** — proving a
control works, not that it exists. It sits exactly where penetration testing, SSDLC
review and automation overlap, which is this team's intersection. Worth watching. Not
worth building before the services business has customers.

## 5. Google Ads API tooling — already screened and killed

Recorded in `MASTER-2026-09-12.md`: *"20+ competitors, **$20/mo floor**, buyer intent
30–140/mo at $25–105 CPC, 5 commercial Google Ads MCP servers already shipping."*
Nothing in the current research changes that.

# Round three — EPAM's channel, e-commerce/integration, operating effectiveness

## How EPAM and the Big 4 actually get customers — and the proof

EPAM sells through *"senior management, sales and business development teams, account
managers and professional staff, with client service professionals and account
managers maintaining direct client relationships."* Globant runs *"a direct
enterprise-sales model… well suited to land and expand: win an initial project,
prove delivery quality, then broaden into adjacent capabilities."*

Neither is a search channel. **And the keyword data proves it empirically:**

| What EPAM sells | Vol/mo | Entry bid | Top bid | YoY |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `custom software development company` | 2,900 | $19.21 | $80.00 | −18% |
| `it outsourcing company` | 880 | $20.00 | $125.76 | −63% |
| `software development outsourcing` | 480 | $43.12 | $165.12 | **−88%** |
| `nearshore software development` | 170 | **$66.98** | **$500.59** | −18% |
| `digital engineering services` | 170 | $22.21 | $40.00 | −22% |

**EPAM books billions. `software development outsourcing` gets 480 searches a month
and is down 88% year on year.** No business of that size is built on this traffic.
`nearshore software development` is the extreme: a $500 top-of-page bid against 170
searches — a handful of queries worth enormous sums, which is exactly why nobody
builds a company on them.

The lesson is not "copy EPAM." It is that **above a certain deal size, search stops
being the channel** and relationships, land-and-expand, partner programmes and
analyst presence take over. Our paid search is for the $10–35k entry engagements;
anything larger arrives through the relationship those create.

this research: `software architecture` counted nine ways, `cruise ship tracker`
historically, and this.
