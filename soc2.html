01 — what readiness means

We get you ready. A licensed firm signs it.

SOC 2 attestation legally requires a licensed CPA firm, and ISO 27001 certification an accredited body. Anyone selling you "a SOC 2 audit" as a consultancy is misrepresenting what they can deliver. What we do is everything up to that point — which is the part that actually takes the months.

ASSESS

Gap assessment

Against the framework and the trust criteria you are actually scoped for, not a generic checklist. Most companies are further along than they think in some areas and nowhere near in others.

BUILD

Controls that operate

Implemented and running, not written into a policy nobody follows. An auditor tests whether the control operated over the period, not whether you documented it.

EVIDENCE

Collection wired into normal work

Evidence gathered as a by-product of how the team already works. The alternative is a scramble every period, forever.

03 — what the platforms do not do

A control that exists is not a control that works

Vanta, Drata and Secureframe are evidence collection and monitoring tools. They connect to your infrastructure and track status. They do not design controls, implement them, fix the cloud misconfiguration they just flagged, or define your access review process. That gap is where the months go.

There is a sharper version of it. Those platforms were built to prove a control exists. They were not built to verify it stops anything — and since the 2022 Trust Services Criteria update, auditors are expected to test operating effectiveness, not merely existence.

the difference
platform says: PASS access reviews are configured an auditor asks: did they run every quarter? did anyone act on a finding? was the leaver actually removed? we test the second set. that is the half a dashboard cannot answer.
We work alongside the platform, not instead of it. If you already run Vanta or Drata, keep it — it does the monitoring well. What we add is the control design, the remediation, and the evidence that the control actually operated.

04 — the new one

ISO 42001 — the AI management standard

Published at the end of 2023, ISO 42001 is to AI systems what ISO 27001 is to information security: a certifiable management system. Enterprise procurement is starting to ask for it the same way it started asking for SOC 2 a decade ago, and for the same reason — somebody needs to be accountable for a system nobody fully understands.

It is early. That is the point. Searches for iso 42001 certification are up 171% year on year and the field of firms who have actually delivered it is small. We are already doing the readiness work for 27001; 42001 is the same motion against a different annex, with the AI guardrails work we were doing anyway underneath it.

what it covers
not model accuracy. governance: · which AI systems exist, and who owns them · impact assessment before deployment · data lineage and provenance · human oversight, and where it binds · monitoring once it is live · incident handling when it is wrong most teams have some of this in a doc somewhere. none of it operating.
Same split as the others. An accredited certification body issues the certificate; we do the readiness. And the honest caveat: this standard is new enough that if nobody is asking you for it, you do not need it yet — we will say so.
