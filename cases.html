02 — field notes / measurement

When the numbers lie

These come from our own operations and research rather than anonymised client work. We made most of them with our own money, which is a different kind of qualification — and it is the list an audit is looking for in your stack.

ATTRIBUTIONseverity: channel-killing

A tracked click is not a delivered click

Our click event fired before the outbound navigation, and the outbound used window.open(). Pop-up blockers silently killed the navigation. In our analytics every click looked healthy. On the partner's side there was nothing at all.

The discrepancy only surfaced because we compared two systems that should have agreed and didn't: 13 pageviews reported by the partner, all from our own office, against real clicks from two different cities in our own data.

An entire acquisition channel appeared to be working and was delivering zero. Fix: outbound links must be real anchors; fire the event on navigation, not before it.
REPORTINGseverity: false diagnosis

The date range that excludes today

A same-day campaign showed zero delivery. The campaign was fine. The query used LAST_7_DAYS, which in the Google Ads API excludes the current day — so a campaign launched that morning was being measured over a window it did not exist in.

A working campaign was nearly paused on a reporting artifact. Any same-day check must use TODAY.
TRACKINGseverity: silent inflation

Conversions that fire before the lead arrives

The common pattern is to fire the conversion tag on form submit. If the form handler then fails — bad endpoint, CORS, a network blip — the ad platform counts a lead that never reached an inbox. Smart Bidding then optimises toward whatever produced those phantom conversions.

Reported CPL falls while real pipeline stays flat. Fix: fire on a confirmed server response, and verify the lead arrives and the conversion registers as two separate checks.

03 — field notes / research

When the data lies

KEYWORD DATAseverity: voided a business case

Headline volumes that were artifacts

A term we had twice flagged as the one open door showed 12,100 searches a month. It was a vendor's brand name — Google normalises it with the branded spelling, and both returned byte-identical rows. Real generic demand was around 800.

The same trap, caught three more times: four "cruise ship tracker" variants that normalise to a single keyword rather than four; three "AI project management" phrasings that collapse into one row; a term whose volume swung 55× month to month with a mis-detected language tag.

Two headline numbers in our own research were artifacts. Standing rule: before trusting any volume, check whether it normalises with a brand term or a synonym.
BID DATAseverity: wrong by multiples

Entry bid is not achieved CPC

Keyword tools report a top-of-page bid. That is what it costs to enter the auction, not what you end up paying. Affordability models built on the wrong field are wrong by a multiple, and always in the optimistic direction.

Every break-even calculation in an early model had to be rebuilt. The two values are now separate fields in our corpus so they cannot be confused again.
UNIT ECONOMICSseverity: 1.8× error

An assumed commission rate

A model assumed roughly 7% commission. Measuring the actual partner catalogue — 1,884 products, rate stated per product — gave a flat 12.5% across every price tier.

Every break-even was 1.8× too pessimistic and several products written off as unviable were fine. Label every number in a model as measured or assumed, and go measure the assumed ones.
INTENTseverity: budget burn

A cheap click usually means the wrong intent

Low CPC on a commercial-looking term is rarely a bargain. Measured traps from our own work: a destination campaign whose cheapest traffic was fans of a band with the same name; another whose cheap clicks were bourbon, ice cream and a cycling race; a third that was medical tourism at a $0.00 bid.

The B2B version is worse, because it inverts. In consulting a cheap click signals a market with no money in it: a term with 4,400 monthly searches and a $15 top-of-page bid has enormous volume and almost nobody willing to pay for the click, while platform-anchored terms run $80–220 because that click closes implementation work.

Read the search terms report on day two, not day seven. And check the SERP composition before bidding: free tools and "salary" in related searches mean researchers, not buyers.
PRODUCT DATAseverity: killed the premise

The pipeline whose best output was a negative

We built a cross-supplier inventory pipeline on the assumption that the same product is widely sold by multiple vendors, so a comparison layer would have something to compare. Once normalised: 1,178 entities, of which only 45 were genuinely multi-supplier.

The product premise did not survive its own data. Better to learn that from a pipeline than from a launch — and the cheapest version of this finding is usually a query, not a build.
